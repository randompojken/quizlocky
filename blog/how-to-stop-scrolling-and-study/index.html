How to stop scrolling and actually study

You sit down to study, open your phone "for a second," and it's 40 minutes later. That's not weak willpower — TikTok is engineered by thousands of people to win exactly that moment. Beating it takes structure, not shame. Here's what actually works, from weakest to strongest.

1. Make your phone boring at the desk

Turn on grayscale (Settings → Accessibility → Display), silence every notification that isn't a human being, and log out of the feeds you fall into. Each of these removes a little of the slot-machine shine. It won't save you alone, but it lowers the pull on everything below.

2. Add physical distance

A phone in another room can't be checked absent-mindedly, because the checking stops being absent-minded — you'd have to stand up. Distance works brilliantly at home and fails completely in the library, on the bus, and everywhere else your phone lives in your pocket. You need something that travels with you.

3. Use a blocker with real friction

App blockers put a wall between the urge and the feed. The plain ones (a timer that blocks apps for an hour) help until the day you override them. The ones that survive add friction you can't wave away — a physical device you left at home, a breathing exercise, or a task to complete. The friction is the feature: the urge passes in about 90 seconds if the app doesn't open instantly.

4. Flip the urge: make scrolling something you earn

This is the strongest version, and it's the one we built QuizLocky around, so bias declared. Instead of fighting the urge to open TikTok, use it: the apps you chose stay locked until you pass a short quiz generated from your own study notes, and passing earns you screen time — study 10 minutes, scroll 20. Every scroll urge becomes a study session trigger. You stop needing discipline, because the habit you already have does the work.

5. Study with active recall, not rereading

While you're fixing the distraction problem, fix the method too: testing yourself beats rereading and highlighting by a wide margin in study after study. Cover your notes and answer from memory, or use flashcards that make you retrieve. If your study sessions are quizzes rather than rereads, twenty focused minutes outworks an hour of highlighting with one eye on your phone.

6. Protect one 25-minute block, then repeat

Don't plan a heroic five-hour session — plan one 25-minute block with your phone handled by any of the methods above, then a five-minute break. Starting is the entire battle. Most people who start a 25-minute block run past it.

Common questions

How do I stop picking up my phone while studying?

Reduce the payoff and add friction: turn on grayscale, kill notifications, put the phone out of arm's reach, and use a blocker so that picking it up leads somewhere unrewarding. The pickup habit fades when it stops paying out.

Are app blockers actually worth it for students?

Yes, with one caveat: a blocker only works if you don't uninstall it in a weak moment. Blockers that add meaning to the unlock (a quiz, a physical action) survive longer than plain timers, because you stop fighting the block and start using it.

How long should a study block be?

Start with 25 minutes fully off the phone, then a 5 minute break. It's short enough to feel possible on a bad day, and most people find they run longer once they've started. The start is the hard part, not the middle.
